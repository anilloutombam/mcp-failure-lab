# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

MCP Failure Lab is committed to an open, welcoming, and harassment-free
community. Everyone may participate regardless of identity, background,
experience, appearance, ability, nationality, religion, or personal
circumstances.

## Expected behavior

Community members are expected to:

- Communicate with empathy, patience, and respect.
- Keep technical disagreement focused on ideas, evidence, and project goals.
- Give constructive feedback and accept it professionally.
- Respect privacy, consent, and reasonable personal boundaries.
- Take responsibility for mistakes and help repair their impact.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable:

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, discrimination, or sustained disruption.
- Sexualized language, imagery, attention, or advances.
- Insults, trolling, personal attacks, or deliberately inflammatory comments.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern in good faith.
- Conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional
  open-source community.

## Scope

This policy applies to repository activity, reviews, issue discussions, project
communication channels, and public situations where someone is representing MCP
Failure Lab.

## Reporting

For an immediate GitHub platform-safety concern, use GitHub's reporting tools.
For a confidential project conduct report, contact the maintainer at
<nlkr50@gmail.com>. Do not open a public issue containing sensitive personal
information.

Reports should include the relevant links, dates, context, and any supporting
material that can be shared safely. Reports will be handled as privately as
practical. Information will be shared only when needed to investigate and act.

Security vulnerabilities must be reported through the process in
[`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md), not through the conduct-reporting channel.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove content, reject contributions, issue a private or
public warning, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban a person
from project spaces. The response will consider the severity, impact, context,
and pattern of behavior. Good-faith reports will not result in retaliation.

Maintainers involved in a report must not make the final enforcement decision
when another uninvolved maintainer is available.
